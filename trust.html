The intelligence model

Four layers—not one generic chatbot.

The competitive advantage comes from combining disciplined workflow, measurable growth operations, human-reviewed intelligence, and protected operational memory.

01 · ACQUISITION

Self-marketing intelligence

Measures which pages, messages, calls to action, and offers produce qualified conversations. AI drafts improvements; approved experiments decide what actually wins.

02 · CONVERSION

Lead and workflow intelligence

Classifies non-confidential inquiry signals, surfaces urgency and fit, recommends a next action, and keeps owner review between recommendation and execution.

03 · OPERATIONS

Case-support intelligence

Assists with chronology extraction, source comparison, unresolved-conflict detection, question generation, and structured drafting while keeping inference separate from fact.

04 · MEMORY

Controlled institutional learning

Approved outcomes, objections, workflow patterns, and product decisions become governed knowledge—not unrestricted model memory or silent production mutation.

05 · SECURITY

Fail-closed authority

Identity, role, organization, case authority, revocation status, and current authorization state remain authoritative even when an AI index or cache is stale.

06 · OWNER CONTROL

Founder approval gates

Commercial terms, legal positions, client acceptance, sensitive access, infrastructure ownership, and public production changes remain subject to Jessie McGhee’s owner controls.

Continuous improvement loop

Measure. Explain. Test. Approve. Promote.

“Self-improving” means a governed release process—not uncontrolled autonomous rewriting.

MeasureCollect approved, low-risk funnel and workflow signals.
ExplainIdentify drop-off, objections, errors, and operational friction.
ProposeGenerate copy, product, and workflow recommendations with rationale.
ValidateRun tests, security checks, and controlled experiments.
PromotePublish only after the required owner or qualified reviewer approval.

What the AI is not allowed to do

  • Invent facts, conceal uncertainty, or present inference as verified evidence.
  • Conduct unlawful surveillance, unauthorized access, harassment, discrimination, or evasion of legal process.
  • Accept a client, promise a result, set or change pricing, or authorize a payment destination without owner approval.
  • Release protected information, alter chain-of-custody records, or bypass role and organization boundaries.
  • Deploy production changes solely because a model generated them.
